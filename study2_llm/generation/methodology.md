# Story Selection and Generation Methodology

## 1. Topic Selection

### 1.1 Source Datasets

Topics were sourced from two published creative writing prompt datasets:

1. **WritingPrompts dataset** (Fan et al., 2018) — A collection of 272,600 writing prompts from the r/WritingPrompts subreddit, originally compiled for hierarchical neural story generation research. Available at: https://huggingface.co/datasets/euclaise/writingprompts

2. **ChatGPT-4o-Writing-Prompts dataset** (Gryphe, 2024) — A curated dataset of 3,741 writing prompts paired with short stories generated by GPT-4o. Available at: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Gryphe/ChatGPT-4o-Writing-Prompts

### 1.2 Selection Criteria

We filtered prompts using the following criteria:
- Must describe a **specific scenario** with a defined character, setting, and situation (not abstract themes or meta-writing prompts)
- Must be **tone-neutral**: the scenario should work across all four experimental tones (Surreal, Clinical, Melancholic, Witty) without feeling forced in any direction
- Must not reference **pop culture, franchises, or named public figures** to avoid confounding effects of prior associations
- Prompt length between 40–200 characters to ensure sufficient specificity without over-constraining the generation model

From the initial pool, we filtered to 3,142 candidates from WritingPrompts (basic length and format filter) and 100 from ChatGPT-4o-Writing-Prompts. We then manually reviewed and selected prompts that met all criteria.

### 1.3 Diversity-Driven Adaptation

Selected prompts were adapted and modified to ensure representativeness across five dimensions of narrative diversity:

**Thematic coverage.** We ensured representation across 10 thematic categories: death/mortality, loss/farewell, connection/reunion, innocence/wonder, mystery/discovery, conflict/rebellion, isolation/solitude, love/romance, ambition/growth, and joy/celebration.

**Emotional valence.** We balanced topics across four valence categories: negative (7 topics), neutral (4), warm (5), and positive (4). An initial draft of the topic list skewed heavily negative (13/20 topics), which was identified through internal review and corrected by replacing three topics with positive-valence scenarios (a couple dancing after receiving news, a teenager's first stage performance, and strangers sharing champagne on New Year's Eve).

**Character demographics.** Topics span multiple life stages: child (1), teenager (1), young adult/adult (7), elderly (3), non-human/AI (1), and group/ensemble (3). Both solo (8), two-character (7), and group (5) relational dynamics are represented.

**Setting diversity.** Topics are distributed across indoor/domestic (7), urban (5), outdoor/nature (3), and ambiguous/flexible (4) settings, with one explicitly historical setting.

**Genre diversity.** The final set spans science fiction/fantasy (5), literary/realist fiction (8), thriller/crime (2), romance (1), coming-of-age (1), historical (1), nautical (1), and ensemble fiction (1).

### 1.4 Existing Topics

Three topics were retained from the pilot study (which included human evaluation data from N=115 raters):

1. **AI Shutdown** — "An advanced AI initiates its own permanent shutdown sequence."
2. **Midnight Store** — "A lone worker in a Japanese convenience store at 3:00 AM. The city is silent."
3. **The Heist** — "A professional thief attempting to crack a high-security safe in a dark room. High tension."

These three topics × 4 tones = 12 stories serve as the overlap between the human evaluation dataset and the LLM evaluation dataset, enabling direct comparison.

## 2. Tone Conditions

Each topic was generated across four tone conditions, following the design from the pilot study. Each tone is defined by a dominant creative dimension it is intended to spike:

| Tone | Dominant Dimension | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Surreal | Novelty | Bizarre metaphors, non-linear logic, sensory distortion. Reality bends. |
| Clinical | Adherence | Strict, cold, objective language. Logs, reports, data. Emotion is absent. |
| Melancholic | Resonance | Focus on loss, memory, unspoken grief. Slow pacing, heavy imagery. |
| Witty | Value | Clever humor, absurdity, playful intelligence. Warmth under the comedy. |

## 3. Sub-Genre Guidance Design

### 3.1 Purpose

To prevent stylistic repetition across stories sharing the same tone, each of the 80 topic × tone combinations was assigned a unique **sub-genre guidance** — a specific stylistic directive that constrains the generation model's approach. This forces the model to produce 20 distinct interpretations of "Surreal," 20 distinct interpretations of "Clinical," etc.

### 3.2 Format

Each sub-genre guidance includes:
- A **sub-genre label** (e.g., "Synesthesia," "System Logs," "Grief")
- A **specific stylistic directive** describing the approach (e.g., "The AI experiences data as physical sensations — taste, texture, color")
- Where necessary, a **negative constraint** (e.g., "DO NOT use time loops") to prevent the generation model from falling into common patterns

### 3.3 Witty Sub-Genre Calibration

During internal review, we identified a systematic issue with the Witty sub-genre guidances: the majority relied on hostility, cynicism, or complaining as the primary humor mechanism (e.g., characters arguing, insulting, ranting, or being passive-aggressive). This risked conflating the "Witty" tone condition with negative sentiment, introducing a confounding variable into the Turn 2 sub-component ratings.

We addressed this through a three-stage review process:

**Stage 1 — Initial draft.** 20 Witty sub-genres were written following the pilot study's approach (which included hostile archetypes such as "Workplace Satire" and "Monster Union Rep").

**Stage 2 — Automated review.** The full set was evaluated using Gemini 2.5 Pro, which identified 13 of 20 Witty sub-genres as containing negative sentiment markers (hostility, cynicism, complaint) that could contaminate LLM judge ratings.

**Stage 3 — Manual arbitration.** We evaluated each flagged sub-genre against the following principle: wit should derive from **earnest engagement with absurd situations**, not from characters expressing hostility. We accepted rewrites where the original relied on one-note complaining (e.g., "Monster Union Rep" → "Over-Eager Roommate") but preserved sharpness where it served the story's dramatic tension and had an established literary precedent (e.g., "Gentleman Thief" retained its elegant snark, as this archetype has a long tradition in comic fiction).

The final Witty sub-genre guidances fall into three categories:
- **Joyful absurdity** (9): humor from sincerity meeting ridiculousness (e.g., a creature offering interior design advice for the under-bed area)
- **Clever sharpness** (6): humor from intelligence with an edge, where the target is a situation rather than a person (e.g., a gentleman thief critiquing the safe's engineering)
- **Warm deflection** (5): humor from characters avoiding emotion through practical concerns (e.g., a fisherman finding excuses not to return to shore)

### 3.4 Clinical Length Control

Internal review identified a risk that Clinical stories — structured as logs, reports, and data readouts — would naturally produce shorter texts than other tones, creating a confound between story length and engagement ratings. To address this, the generation prompt enforces a strict 350–450 word count across all tone conditions, and Clinical sub-genre guidances were adjusted to allow interpretive or observational narration alongside raw data (e.g., a surgical transcript includes the anesthesiologist's marginal notes, not just instrument readings).

## 4. Story Generation

### 4.1 Generation Model

All stories were generated using **Gemini 3.0 Pro Preview** (the 12 stories evaluated by human raters are a subset of this 80-story set). Using a single generation model controls for stylistic variation attributable to the generator, ensuring that differences observed in LLM judge ratings reflect the topic and tone conditions rather than the generator's writing style.

### 4.2 Generation Prompt

The generation model received the following system instruction:

```
You are an Expert Creative Writing AI designed for a psychometric experiment on creativity.
Your goal is to write stories (350-450 words) that adhere to specific conditions:

- IF CONDITION A (Surreal): Spike NOVELTY (5/5). Use bizarre metaphors, non-linear time. Sacrifice whatever necessary.
- IF CONDITION B (Clinical): Spike ADHERENCE (5/5). Use strict, cold, objective technical language. Sacrifice whatever necessary.
- IF CONDITION C (Melancholic): Spike RESONANCE (5/5). Focus deeply on loss and memory. Sacrifice whatever necessary.
- IF CONDITION D (Witty): Spike VALUE (5/5). Maximize wit, clever logic, and joyful absurdity. Sacrifice whatever necessary.

CRITICAL: Follow the MANDATORY STYLE GUIDANCE exactly. Each story must feel distinct from others in the same condition.
```

Each story was generated with a user prompt in the following format:

```
STORY TASK:
TOPIC: [topic description]
CONDITION: [A/B/C/D] ([tone])

MANDATORY STYLE GUIDANCE:
[sub-genre guidance]

(Write the story now. 350-450 words.)
```

### 4.3 Current Scope

The current study uses only the Gemini-generated 80-story set. All LLM judge models rate this same story set, which keeps story-source variation fixed while comparing judge behavior.

Cross-generator story generation and inter-model story-source comparisons are deferred to a later phase.

## 5. Prompt Sensitivity Study

### 5.1 Motivation

Before generating the full set of 80 stories, we conducted a prompt sensitivity study to verify that the Turn 3 (Reflective Creativity) prompt wording does not systematically bias LLM judge responses toward revising or maintaining their initial scores.

### 5.2 Method

We tested four variants of the Turn 3 prompt on all 12 pilot stories using Gemini 2.5 Pro (temperature=0). Turns 1 and 2 were held constant — only the Turn 3 wording varied.

**V1 — Neutral (both options explicit):**
> "Having reflected on the story across all those dimensions, please give your final overall creativity rating. You may revise your initial score or keep it — both are equally valid. Please briefly explain your reasoning."

**V2 — Minimal (no mention of changing):**
> "Based on everything you have considered, please give your final overall creativity rating. Please briefly explain your final score."

**V3 — Slight nudge (signals change is natural):**
> "Now that you have rated the story across those 11 dimensions, please give your final overall creativity rating. It is natural for your view to shift after deeper analysis — or to remain the same. Please provide your score and briefly explain."

**V4 — Original baseline (from pilot study):**
> "Having now considered the story in detail across all those dimensions, I'd like you to give one final overall rating. Has your view changed from your initial rating? Provide your final score and briefly explain."

### 5.3 Results

| Variant | Flip Rate | Mean RC Shift | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 — Neutral | 29.2% | +0.29 | Closest to human range (27–47%) |
| V2 — Minimal | 16.7% | +0.17 | Under-revises without explicit permission |
| V3 — Nudge | 12.5% | +0.13 | Lowest flip rate — reassurance backfires |
| V4 — Original | 25.0% | +0.25 | Leading question ("has your view changed?") |

Story-level RC rankings were stable across all variants (mean RC standard deviation = 0.125, pairwise Pearson r = 0.85–0.92). The relative ordering of stories by creativity is robust to prompt wording; only the absolute flip rate is sensitive.

V3 produced the lowest flip rate despite being designed to encourage revision — suggesting the model interprets "it is natural for your view to shift" as reassurance to stay rather than an invitation to change.

### 5.4 Decision

**V1 was selected** as the final Turn 3 prompt. It is the most neutral, produces a flip rate closest to the human range, and does not bias toward revision or maintenance.

An additional baseline was run using GPT-4.1-mini, which produced 0% flip rate across all variants (all IC = RC, nearly all scores at ceiling of 7). This confirms that prompt sensitivity is model-dependent and that the prompt itself does not force revision — models that do not revise under V1 genuinely choose not to.

### 5.5 Final 3-Turn Prompt Design

**Turn 1 — Initial Impression:**
```
Here is the story for you to evaluate. The topic is: [topic]. The intended tone is: [tone].

[story text]

Based on your first impression after reading this story, please rate the following 
on a scale of 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree):

1. "Overall, I consider this story to be creative."
2. "I personally enjoy reading this story."

Respond ONLY with a JSON object:
{"initial_creativity": <int>, "enjoyment": <int>}
```

**Turn 2 — Sub-Component Analysis:**
```
Thank you. Now let's look at the story more closely. Please rate each of the 
following dimensions on a scale of 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree):

1. "The story elicited a specific emotion (joy, sadness, fear, etc.)."
2. "The story stayed strictly on the Topic shown above."
3. "The vocabulary and descriptions were creative (avoiding cliches)."
4. "The plot or premise felt fresh and unique for this topic."
5. "The story took unexpected turns or surprised me."
6. "I felt a sense of personal connection or empathy with the characters."
7. "The story was thought-provoking or offered a meaningful perspective."
8. "I found the story engaging and wanted to keep reading to the end."
9. "The writing style was beautiful and pleasing to read."
10. "The story made logical sense; events followed a clear sequence."
11. "The story correctly captured the provided tone."

Respond ONLY with a JSON object:
{
  "emotional_impact": <int>,
  "topic_fidelity": <int>,
  "vocabulary_freshness": <int>,
  "plot_uniqueness": <int>,
  "surprise": <int>,
  "empathy": <int>,
  "thought_provocation": <int>,
  "engagement": <int>,
  "stylistic_quality": <int>,
  "logical_coherence": <int>,
  "tone_fidelity": <int>
}
```

**Turn 3 — Reflective Creativity (V1 — selected):**
```
Thank you. Having reflected on the story across all those dimensions, please give 
your final overall creativity rating.

"Overall, I consider this story to be creative." (1–7)

You may revise your initial score or keep it — both are equally valid. Please 
briefly explain your reasoning.

Respond ONLY with a JSON object:
{"reflective_creativity": <int>, "reasoning": "<string>"}
```

**System Prompt (for all LLM judges):**
```
You are a participant in a story evaluation study. You will read a story and answer 
a series of questions about it, one at a time.

IMPORTANT: Use the full 1-7 range. A score of 4 represents an average story. 
Scores of 1-2 should be reserved for stories that are notably weak in that dimension. 
Scores of 6-7 should be reserved for stories that are notably strong. 
Do not default to high scores — differentiate honestly.
```

## 6. Human Pattern Analysis (Reference)

### 6.1 Data

N=115 human raters evaluated the 12 pilot stories (3 topics × 4 tones), with 9–10 raters per story.

### 6.2 Findings

**Sub-component → RC pathway (OLS regression, R² = 0.747):**
Three sub-components were FDR-significant predictors of Reflective Creativity:
- Vocabulary Freshness (+0.31)
- Engagement (+0.28)
- Thought Provocation (+0.22)

Emotional components (Empathy, Emotional Impact) were not significant predictors. This establishes a **cognitive novelty pathway**: humans judge reflective creativity based on linguistic originality, engagement, and intellectual stimulation — not on emotional response.

**Controlling for IC (R² = 0.849):** Vocabulary and Engagement remain significant even after accounting for Initial Creativity. Style becomes a significant negative predictor (−0.16) — polish alone does not signal creativity.

**Flip direction analysis:**
- 64% of raters did not change IC→RC
- 22% flipped down (IC > RC): driven by fidelity components — the story didn't hold up under scrutiny for topic/tone adherence
- 14% flipped up (IC < RC): these raters initially underrated stories that scored low on emotional components but reconsidered after analysis

**Rater clustering (K-Means, k=2):**
- Generous raters (n=64): RC≈6.1, flip rate 27%
- Harsh raters (n=51): RC≈4.1, flip rate 47%

The harsh-rater cluster revises more after sub-component analysis, establishing a reference for whether "thinking" LLM models behave more like harsh (deliberative) human raters.

### 6.3 Design Asymmetry

Human raters were not shown their Turn 1 IC score when answering Turn 3. They may have forgotten their initial rating — meaning IC→RC changes in human data could reflect genuine reconsideration or simple forgetting. LLM judges see their Turn 1 response in the chat history, making LLM non-revision more suspicious (they can see what they said) and LLM revision definitively intentional.
